Drawing a house and garden with SVG

The picture uses SVG primitives and a shared window group to demonstrate shapes, coordinates, fills, strokes and transforms.

Step 1 and Step 3

Customised SVG picture

The SVG uses a 1000 × 700 coordinate system. Its origin, (0,0), is at the top-left; x increases to the right and y increases downwards.

Step 2

Annotated coordinate example

The annotations connect visible points to the attributes used in the SVG code.

House rectangle

<rect x="325" y="315" width="390" height="235" />

The first point is the top-left corner. Width and height extend right and down.

Sun circle

<circle cx="850" cy="105" r="58" />

The centre is (850,105). The radius extends 58 units from the centre.

Cloud ellipse

<ellipse cx="160" cy="105" rx="70" ry="30" />

rx is the horizontal radius; ry is the vertical radius.

Fence line

<line x1="790" y1="465" x2="790" y2="560" />

A line joins its start and end points. Both x values are equal, so this line is vertical.

Chimney cap

<polyline points="620,154 652,128 685,154" />

The points are joined in order. A polyline does not automatically close its outline.

Roof polygon

<polygon points="285,320 520,120 755,320" />

The final point connects back to the first to form a closed triangle.

Garden path

<path d="M 500 700 C 430 625, 455 570, 535 510" />

M sets the start. C gives two control points followed by the end point; the curve need not pass through the control points.

Heading text

<text x="520" y="92" text-anchor="middle">Welcome Home</text>

The anchor lies on the text baseline. Middle alignment centres the text horizontally at x=520.
